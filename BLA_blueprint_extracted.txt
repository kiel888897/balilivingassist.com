Bali Living Assist (BLA)
 SYSTEM BLUEPRINT v1.0
 Property Service  Supply  Rental Platform
Purpose: This blueprint defines the business model, website structure, customer journey, admin panel,
modules, transaction workflows, data architecture, bilingual approach, and implementation phases for BLA. It is
intended as the functional reference before database and Laravel development.
Core positioning: BLA is a property service, procurement, supply and rental company for villa owners and
expatriates across Bali. BLA sources products from suppliers and coordinates projects through external
vendors.


--- PAGE 2 ---
1. Business Model
 Target customers: Villa owners and expatriates in Bali.
 Coverage: Entire Bali, with BLA-owned delivery.
 Supply model: Products are sourced from suppliers rather than relying on large owned inventory.
 Project model: BLA manages projects and coordinates external vendors.
 Products: Ready-made and custom products.
 Rental: Tools and equipment; a product can be sold, rented, or both.
 Payment v1: Customer places an order/request → admin confirms → customer makes manual bank transfer
→ admin records/validates payment.
 Revenue: Commission, agent/broker arrangements, percentage-based margin, and related service/project
arrangements.
2. Core Business Areas
 SHOP: Building materials, electronics, furniture, tools and other configurable product categories.
 SERVICES: Construction, renovation, interior, electrical, plumbing, AC, painting, pool, garden, cleaning,
general maintenance and any future service categories.
 RENTAL: Rental of tools/equipment. Initial categories: Air Compressors and Air Tools; Compaction; Concrete;
Material Handling; Power & Hand Tools; Scaffolding and Ladders; Vehicles; Welding.
 PROJECTS: Multi-step work coordinated by BLA and external vendors.
 REQUEST ASSISTANCE: General lead/request channel for needs that do not fit a standard product or
service.
3. Product Master Concept
 A single master Product record can support both sale and rental. This avoids duplicate records when the same
item is both sold and rented.
 Availability flags: For Sale, For Rental. Either, both, or neither while inactive.
 Sale pricing: Fixed Price, Request Price, or Custom.
 Rental pricing: Initially daily pricing; architecture should allow hourly, weekly and monthly pricing later.
 Rental-specific data: rental price, pricing unit, security deposit, rentable quantity, availability/reservation
rules.
 Customer visibility: Customers see only the final BLA price. Supplier cost, internal margin, commission and
vendor cost remain internal/admin data.
 Categories: Product categories and Rental categories are managed separately, but a single product may be
linked to both.
4. Product Detail UX
 A rental-enabled product detail page should show a compact sale section and rental section when both are
enabled.
 Example: Concrete Mixer — Buy: Rp25,000,000; Rent: Rp350,000/day. Rental section includes start date,
end date, duration, availability check and Add Rental to Cart.
 Sale CTA: Quantity + Add to Cart.

 Rental CTA: Start Date + End Date + duration/availability + Add Rental to Cart.
 Mixed cart: The same product can appear once as SALE and once as RENTAL because cart items carry a
transaction type.
5. Homepage Blueprint
 Header: Logo; Shop; Services; Rental; Projects; About; language EN/ID; Login; Cart.
 Hero: 3-slide banner for Shop, Services and Rental, each with image, bilingual text, CTA and link.
 Shop by Category: Dynamic product categories configured by admin.
 Brand/Promotional separator: Property-focused message such as “Everything Your Property Needs. One
Assist.” with CTA.
 Services: Dynamic service categories configured by admin.
 Assistance CTA: “Need Something Specific?” → Request Assistance.
 Rental: Dynamic rental categories.
 How BLA Works: Tell Us → Source & Coordinate → Confirm Price → Deliver / Complete.
 Footer: Brand, Shop, Services, Rental, Company, Contact, language, legal links.
6. Customer Website
 Public: Home, Shop, Product Detail, Services, Service Detail, Rental, Rental Product Detail, Projects, About,
Contact, Request Assistance, Cart, Login/Register, legal pages.
 Customer account: Dashboard, Orders, Quotations, Projects, Rentals, Service Requests, Invoices,
Payments, Addresses, Profile.
 Customer principle: Keep the account focused on the customer’s transactions and requests; never expose
internal supplier cost, vendor cost or commission.
7. Admin Panel
 Dashboard: Orders, new requests, quotations, pending payments, active projects, rental bookings, sales and
BLA commission.
 Website: Homepage management, hero slides, promotional sections, menus, translations, banners and
content.
 Catalog: Products, product categories, brands, images, pricing, sale/rental availability.
 Services: Service categories, services, requests, scheduling.
 Rental: Rental categories, rental-enabled products, availability, bookings, deposits, returns.
 Commerce: Carts, orders, quotations, invoices, payments.
 Operations: Projects, tasks, vendors, suppliers, delivery.
 Finance: Internal cost, commission/margin, payment records and reports.
 Users: Customer and admin users, roles and permissions.
 Settings: Languages, delivery rates, business settings, notification templates and other configuration.
8. Admin Roles & Permissions
 Customer: Own account and transactions only.


--- PAGE 4 ---
 Super Admin: Full system access.
 Sales: Customers, requests, quotations, orders and customer communication.
 Product Manager: Products, categories, suppliers and catalog management.
 Project Manager: Service requests, projects, tasks, vendors and schedules.
 Rental Manager: Rental catalog, availability, bookings, deposits and returns.
 Finance: Invoices, payments, commissions and financial reporting.
 Roles should be permission-driven. Menus are generated from permissions so each login sees only relevant
sections.
9. Core Transaction Workflows
 Fixed-price sale: Product → Cart → Checkout → Order → Admin confirmation → Awaiting payment →
Manual bank transfer → Payment verification → Processing → Delivery → Completed.
 Request-price/custom: Product/Service → Request Quote → Admin review → Supplier/vendor sourcing →
Internal cost → BLA final price → Quotation → Customer acceptance → Order → Payment → Fulfillment.
 Service: Request Assistance/Service → Admin review → Vendor coordination → Quotation → Acceptance →
Payment → Schedule → Vendor assigned → In progress → Completed.
 Rental: Rental product → Dates → Availability → Cart → Order → Admin confirmation → Payment/deposit →
Reserved → Out/Rented → Return → Deposit handling → Completed.
 Mixed order: One order can contain SALE and RENTAL items and service-related items. Each item retains its
transaction type and operational requirements.
10. Rental Rules
 Rental availability must be date-aware. A product can have a rentable quantity and overlapping reservations
must reduce availability.
 Rental item should support start date, end date, duration, unit price, deposit and delivery.
 Future pricing units can include hourly, daily, weekly and monthly.
 Rental return should record returned date, condition, damage/notes and deposit outcome where applicable.
 Sale inventory and rental availability are operationally different; do not assume a sale quantity is automatically
rentable.
11. Cart & Checkout
 Cart item should include product, transaction type (SALE/RENTAL), quantity and pricing snapshot.
 Rental cart item additionally stores rental start/end dates, duration and rental price snapshot.
 Service/custom items may enter the quotation flow rather than direct checkout when a final price is not known.
 Cart can contain fixed-price sale items and rental items together. If a custom/request-price item requires
quotation, the system should clearly route the customer to Request Quote rather than pretending it has a
checkout price.
12. Delivery
 BLA uses its own delivery across Bali.
 Delivery calculation should support distance, weight and volume.


--- PAGE 5 ---
 Admin should be able to configure delivery rates and override the calculated amount when necessary.
 Recommended conceptual model: base fee + distance component + weight component + volume component,
with optional zones/overrides later.
 Delivery should be attached to the relevant order/fulfillment and have its own status.
13. Bilingual Architecture
 Languages: English (EN) and Indonesian (ID).
 Use translation records rather than hard-coded name_en/name_id fields wherever practical.
 Core translatable entities: products, categories, services, rental categories, homepage slides/sections, static
pages, FAQs and future content.
 Customer language preference can be stored for logged-in customers; session/cookie can handle guests.
 Prices, SKU, internal IDs and transaction numbers remain language-neutral.
 Admin should be able to see translation completeness and edit English/Indonesian content.
14. Suggested Data Model
 Identity: users, roles, permissions, role_user, permission_role.
 Customer: customers, addresses.
 Catalog: products, product_translations, product_images, product_categories, categories,
category_translations, brands (optional), product_prices.
 Services: service_categories, service_category_translations, services, service_translations,
service_requests.
 Rental: rental_categories, rental_category_translations, rental_settings/product rental fields, rental_bookings,
rental_booking_items, rental_returns.
 Commerce: carts, cart_items, quotations, quotation_items, orders, order_items, invoices, invoice_items,
payments.
 Operations: projects, project_tasks, project_files/notes, vendors, vendor_jobs, suppliers,
purchase_requests/orders.
 Delivery: delivery_rates, deliveries.
 Finance: commission_rules, order_commissions/earnings, internal_costs.
 Website: hero_slides, homepage_sections, pages, page_translations, menus/menu_items.
 System: notifications, activity_logs, settings.
15. Important Database Principles
 Do not duplicate a product just because it can be sold and rented. Use one Product master with sale/rental
capabilities.
 Keep customer price separate from internal supplier cost and BLA commission/margin.
 Store transaction pricing as snapshots on quotation/order items so historical orders do not change when the
product price changes later.
 Use status fields consistently and use timestamps/audit information for operational traceability.
 Categories and homepage content should be admin-managed, not hard-coded.


--- PAGE 6 ---
 Design for future vendors/suppliers without requiring them to have their own portal in v1.
16. Status Model
 Order: DRAFT → PENDING_CONFIRMATION → CONFIRMED → AWAITING_PAYMENT → PAID →
PROCESSING → READY/DELIVERING → COMPLETED; CANCELLED as an alternate terminal path.
 Quotation: DRAFT → SENT → VIEWED → ACCEPTED or REJECTED; EXPIRED where applicable.
 Service Request: NEW → UNDER_REVIEW → QUOTATION → APPROVED → SCHEDULED →
IN_PROGRESS → COMPLETED; CANCELLED where applicable.
 Rental: REQUESTED → CONFIRMED → AWAITING_PAYMENT → PAID → RESERVED → OUT →
RETURNED → COMPLETED; CANCELLED where applicable.
 Payment: PENDING → SUBMITTED → VERIFIED/PAID or REJECTED.
17. Request Assistance
 This is a central BLA lead/request mechanism for anything not easily purchased as a standard product.
 Suggested fields: customer/contact, property location, request category, description, budget (optional),
preferred date, photos/files, urgency and notes.
 Admin converts the request into a quotation, service job, project, product sourcing task or other appropriate
workflow.
 Example: “Renovate my 3-bedroom villa” can become a project with vendors, supplier items, quotation,
payment milestones and tasks.
18. Project Management
 Projects represent larger or multi-step work such as renovation, construction or major maintenance.
 Project data can include customer, property/location, scope, quotation, vendor(s), tasks, schedule, progress,
documents/photos, costs and customer-facing status.
 Customer should see high-level progress, documents and invoices without seeing internal vendor cost or BLA
margin.
19. Supplier & Vendor Management
 Supplier: Primarily provides products/materials.
 Vendor: Performs services/work.
 Supplier/vendor records should include contact information, categories, service/product coverage, operating
areas, status, notes and internal performance data.
 v1 can be admin-only management; supplier/vendor portal login can be a future phase.
20. Homepage / Content Management
 Admin-managed hero slides: image, EN/ID title, EN/ID description, CTA label, CTA link, sort order and active
status.
 Admin-managed homepage category visibility: show/hide and sort order for Shop, Service and Rental
categories.
 Admin-managed promotional sections: image, EN/ID title, EN/ID description, CTA, link, order and active
status.


--- PAGE 7 ---
 Homepage should be data-driven so content changes do not require code changes.
21. Notifications
 Customer notifications should cover quotation sent, quotation accepted, order confirmed, payment
confirmation, service schedule, rental confirmation, delivery status and completion.
 Initial implementation can use email and/or WhatsApp manually/through future integration; the system should
keep notification events independent of the provider.
22. Security & Access
 Customers can access only their own customer records, addresses, quotations, orders, projects, rentals,
invoices and payments.
 Admin access is permission-based.
 Supplier cost, vendor cost, commission and internal notes are never exposed to customers.
 Important admin actions should be logged, especially price changes, order status changes, payment
verification and commission/cost changes.
23. Recommended Development Phases
 Phase 1 — Foundation: authentication, roles/permissions, bilingual framework, product/category
management, service/category management, rental category management, homepage management.
 Phase 2 — Commerce: product detail, sale pricing, rental pricing, availability, cart, mixed cart, checkout,
order management and manual payment.
 Phase 3 — Quotation: request price, custom products, service requests, quotations, customer acceptance
and invoice flow.
 Phase 4 — Operations: vendors, suppliers, projects, tasks, schedules and delivery calculation.
 Phase 5 — Rental operations: reservations, deposits, returns, condition tracking and rental reporting.
 Phase 6 — Advanced: customer notifications, reporting, vendor/supplier portals, online payment integration,
advanced inventory/procurement and accounting integrations.
24. Technology Direction
 Preferred future stack: Laravel + Tailwind CSS, as planned by the owner.
 Technology should be selected/confirmed after the functional blueprint and database/ERD are approved.
 Recommended implementation principle: build modularly around Catalog, Commerce, Services, Rental,
Projects, Delivery, Finance and Website Management rather than one large monolithic feature set.
25. MVP Acceptance Criteria
 Admin can create/edit/deactivate product categories without code changes.
 Admin can create a product that is sale-only, rental-only or both.
 Customer can see a sale price and rental section on the same product detail page when both are enabled.
 Customer can add sale and rental versions of the same product to one cart.
 Rental dates are checked against availability.
 Customer can request price for uncertain/custom products.


--- PAGE 8 ---
 Admin can create a quotation with a final BLA price while internal costs remain hidden.
 Customer can accept a quotation.
 Admin can confirm an order and record manual bank transfer payment.
 Delivery can be calculated/configured using distance, weight and volume with admin override.
 Homepage categories, hero slides and promotional sections are admin-managed.
 Customer and admin menus are permission/role appropriate.
 English and Indonesian content are supported across core customer-facing content.
